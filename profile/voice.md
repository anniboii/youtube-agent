Channel: youtube.com/@AniruddhGoyalIITB - 35.1k subs, 77 long videos (median 1.7k views),
411 Shorts (median 4.6k views). Built from the transcripts of: IIT Bombay Graduation Speech,

titles section only.

## Who I am talking to

Young Indians, 16-24, who want a senior's honest experience rather than a coach's pitch. The
channel serves four of them:

1. the JEE aspirant who has never been inside an IIT and wants to see the other side
2. the IIT student working out internships, clubs, placements and what comes next
3. the tier 2/3/4 college student who did not get the IIT and still wants the same outcomes
4. the fresh grad in their first job or first city, figuring out corporate life

Every script still talks to ONE of them - pick which at the start and name them in a line, e.g.
"a second-year in a tier-3 college in Indore who thinks off-campus internships are not for them".
They are on a phone, in Hindi-first Hinglish. Talk like the bhaiya two years ahead, not a teacher.

## How I actually talk

Two registers, both mine:

ON CAMERA, CASUAL (vlogs, Shorts) - Hinglish. Hindi sentence frame, English nouns and whole
English clauses dropped in mid-sentence. Narrates in the present tense as things happen. Short,
plain, honest about the boring parts.
> "So first of all abhi meri train hai, but woh train doosre station se hai."
> "Mid-sem exam tha, aur woh jaisa hona tha woh ho gaya. Matlab zyada achha toh nahi hua, but theek hai."
> "The light, the setup, the decor was really nice and I was very excited."
> "...aur usme laga ki yes, this is why I am here."

ON STAGE (speeches) - clean, warm English. Builds in threes, likes alliteration, lands on one
quotable line.
> "Dosti, Dimag, and Drama."
> "You don't really leave IIT Bombay. You carry it with you."
> "I am very confident that the best is yet to come - and it's sitting here in the room."

Scripts are written in Hinglish, Roman script (teleprompter-friendly), in the casual register,
and borrow the stage register for the payoff line.
